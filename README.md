### 02 — FiveM, PC Checking & Anti-Cheat Research

FiveM investigations combine technical knowledge with careful interpretation of system artifacts. My interest is in **permission-based** PC checking, fair-play review processes, and reliable evidence handling.

**Topics of interest:**

- Anti-cheat tooling and workflow design
- Windows artifact analysis
- Process and file metadata inspection
- Investigation timelines and report writing
- Distinguishing observations from assumptions
- Privacy-conscious, consent-based checks

A suspicious artifact is not automatically proof of cheating. Findings should be documented, contextualized, and reviewed fairly.

### 03 — Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT)

OSINT is about gathering and analyzing information from **lawfully accessible public sources**. The hard part is not collecting the most data; it is evaluating whether the information is accurate, relevant, and responsibly used.

**Topics of interest:**

- Public-source research and verification
- Domain and website analysis
- Username and public-profile correlation
- Metadata interpretation
- Research automation
- Source validation and reporting

I avoid presenting unverified claims as facts and do not support harassment, stalking, or disclosure of private information.

### 04 — Digital Forensics

Digital forensics is the process of understanding what available artifacts can—and cannot—tell us about an event.

**Topics of interest:**

- File metadata and integrity
- Hashing and verification
- Event and activity timelines
- Log interpretation
- Evidence preservation
- Technical report structure

A good investigation preserves context, documents limitations, and keeps evidence handling transparent.

## `> cat workflow.md`

### How I approach a project

**1. Define the problem.** Identify the user, the goal, and the constraints before choosing tools.

**2. Research the environment.** Understand the software, platform, file formats, APIs, and relevant documentation.

**3. Build a minimal prototype.** Test the core behavior before adding complexity.

**4. Validate the results.** Compare expected and observed behavior; investigate failures rather than hiding them.

**5. Document the work.** Explain setup, limitations, assumptions, and known issues.

**6. Improve iteratively.** Refactor, test, and revise based on evidence and feedback.

## `> cat research-standards.md`

My work and interests touch subjects that can affect other people. Responsible boundaries are important:

- **Authorization first:** Only inspect devices, accounts, servers, or systems with permission.
- **Privacy by design:** Avoid collecting or exposing personal information unnecessarily.
- **Evidence over accusations:** Separate facts, hypotheses, and unknowns.
- **Reproducibility:** Describe methods clearly enough for legitimate review.
- **Responsible disclosure:** Report vulnerabilities through appropriate channels.
- **Respect for communities:** Avoid using modifications or investigations to harass or disrupt others.

These principles are especially relevant to PC checking, anti-cheat investigations, and OSINT.
